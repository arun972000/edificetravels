The short version: pack less than you think, cover your shoulders and knees, bring layers, and wear shoes you can slip off in a second. Laundry in Indian hotels is quick and good, and anything you've forgotten can be bought in any town. What follows is the longer version, built from the questions our guests ask most.

## Documents

- **Passport,** valid for at least six months from the day you arrive in India, with two blank pages.
- **Your e-Visa approval,** printed. Immigration officers sometimes ask for the paper copy. Our e-Visa guide explains how to apply.
- **Travel insurance details,** with the emergency number somewhere you can find it without your phone.
- **Copies** of your passport and visa, on paper and on your phone, kept separately from the originals.
- **Your itinerary and our contacts,** which we send before you travel. Your driver's name and number will be on it.

## Clothes for the season

**October to March, north and west India.** Days are warm and clear; mornings and nights are cool, and from December to February they're cold. Delhi in January can drop to five degrees at night, and the Thar desert close to freezing. Bring a fleece or light down jacket, a scarf and closed shoes alongside your lighter clothes.

**October to March, south India.** Warm to hot and often humid. Light cotton and linen, a sun hat, and one long-sleeved layer for air-conditioned cars, trains and the evenings in Munnar or Coorg, which are cooler.

**April to June.** Hot almost everywhere on the plains, past 40 degrees in the north. Loose, light-coloured clothes that cover you are cooler than shorts, and a wide hat matters. The hill stations are mild.

**June to September.** The monsoon, except in Ladakh. A light rain jacket or a good umbrella, quick-drying clothes, sandals that can get wet, and zip bags for your phone and papers.

**Ladakh and the high Himalaya.** Even in summer the sun is fierce and the nights near freezing. A down jacket, a warm hat, gloves, sunglasses, high-factor sunscreen and lip balm. Layers are everything.

## Clothes for the places

- **Shoulders and knees covered** for temples, mosques, gurdwaras and villages, for men and women alike.
- **A scarf or light shawl,** which every traveller ends up using: for heads in gurdwaras, shoulders in temples, dust on the road and cold air in the car.
- **Slip-on shoes or sandals.** You'll take them off many times a day. Socks are a kindness on hot stone floors in the afternoon.
- **One smarter outfit** for the palace hotels and good restaurants, which are relaxed but not casual.
- **Swimwear** for hotel pools and the beach, never for the street.

Women often find it easiest to buy a couple of long kurtas in the first city. They're cool, inexpensive and right for every situation. Our dress and etiquette guide has more on what to wear where.

## Health and comfort

- **Prescription medicines** in their original packaging, with a copy of the prescription, and enough for the whole trip and a few days more.
- **A small kit:** rehydration salts, an anti-diarrhoea remedy your doctor recommends, painkillers, plasters, antiseptic and anything for motion sickness if mountain roads trouble you.
- **Insect repellent** with DEET or picaridin, especially for the monsoon, the backwaters and the forests.
- **Sunscreen and sunglasses.** Indian sun is strong at every altitude.
- **Hand sanitiser and tissues,** because roadside toilets don't always have soap or paper.
- **Earplugs and an eye mask,** for temple bells at dawn, dogs at night and early flights.
- **A refillable bottle.** Every one of our vehicles carries filtered water for refills.

See a doctor or travel clinic six to eight weeks before you go about vaccinations and whether your route needs malaria tablets. Our food and staying well guide covers the rest.

## Gadgets

- **A universal adapter.** India uses plug types C, D and M at 230 volts. Hotels often have universal sockets; older buildings and homestays don't.
- **A power bank** for long drive days.
- **A small torch or headlamp,** useful in village homestays and during the occasional power cut.
- **A phone that's unlocked,** if you want an Indian SIM or eSIM. Our money and connectivity guide explains how.

## Bags

A soft-sided suitcase or duffel is easiest in car boots and on domestic flights, which usually allow 15 kilograms of checked luggage in economy. Bring a small daypack for the days out, with room for water, a scarf and a layer.

## What to leave at home

- **Drones.** Foreign visitors need government permission to fly one, monuments ban them, and customs may hold them until you leave.
- **Satellite phones,** which are illegal in India without a licence.
- **Valuable jewellery** you would miss.
- **Too many clothes.** Hotel laundry is quick, usually back the same day, and you'll want the space for what you buy.

## What to buy there

Scarves, kurtas, sandals, toiletries, a SIM card and anything you've forgotten. Your guide or driver will know a fair shop, and you'll be glad you left the room.
