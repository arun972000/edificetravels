Thekkady sits at 900 metres on the border of Kerala and Tamil Nadu, at the top of the Cardamom Hills. The name covers two places: Periyar Tiger Reserve, 925 square kilometres of evergreen forest and grassland around an artificial lake, and Kumily, the spice-trading village at its gate, where the hotels are. The lake was made in 1895, when the British dammed the Periyar river to irrigate the Tamil plains. It drowned a valley, and the dead tree trunks still stand in the water like a flooded orchard. The forest around it became a reserve in 1934 and a tiger reserve in 1978.

Our honest advice about Periyar is not to come for tigers. You won't see one; we almost never do. The elephants are another matter. There are around a thousand, and they come down to the lake in family groups in the early morning and late afternoon. You may also see gaur, the huge dark bison of Indian forests, and lion-tailed macaques and Malabar giant squirrels in the canopy. Great hornbills sound like a steam train when they fly over. Best of all is walking in the forest with one of the reserve's guides, which few big reserves in India allow.

Two nights gives you a forest walk one morning and the first boat the next, with the spice gardens and a Kalaripayattu performance in between. Keep the second afternoon free. Kumily is cool, there's decent coffee in the village, and the pepper and cardamom shops around the bus stand sell the real thing. Thekkady fits neatly between Munnar and the backwaters, three and a half hours from the tea and four from the water. Pack a jumper; after Munnar, it's the coolest stop on a Kerala route.
