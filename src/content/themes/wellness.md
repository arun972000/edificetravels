People come to India to rest in two quite different ways, and it helps to know which one you want before you book. One is Ayurveda as medicine: a course of treatment prescribed by a doctor, with a strict diet and daily therapies, over two or three weeks in a retreat that is closer to a clinic than a spa. The other is a gentler reset: yoga in the mornings, good food, long walks and nothing urgent, somewhere green and quiet.

Kerala is the home of the first. The tradition here is written down, taught in colleges and practised in hospitals, and the best centres are run by families of physicians going back generations. We use a small number of them, in the hills and on the coast, and we only send guests for panchakarma when they have the full fortnight it needs.

Rishikesh is the home of the second. The Ganges comes out of the mountains here, still cold and green, past ashrams that have taught yoga to visitors for decades. Mornings are for classes, evenings for the aarti on the river, and the town has no meat and no alcohol, which makes it easier to keep to a routine.

## How we plan a wellness journey

We start by asking what you want to come home with. A course of treatment needs the right centre and the right season, and very little else around it. A reset can be built into a wider route: a few days on a tea estate, a night on the backwaters, a week of yoga and a beach to finish. Either way, we keep the drives short and leave days empty.

## Who it suits

Solo travellers, couples and friends travelling together, and anyone tired of holidays that need a holiday afterwards. It works well as the second half of a journey that begins with the forts and cities of the north.
