First-time visitors are often told to save Kerala for later. We think that's backwards. Life moves slowly here, the food is some of the best in India, and the distances are short enough that you're never in the car for more than half a day. As a first taste of the country, it's a lot gentler than Delhi.

The route runs north to south in a line. It starts with two nights in Fort Kochi, where the Portuguese, the Dutch and the British each left churches and warehouses on the same peninsula. Then up to Munnar at 1,600 metres, where the tea estates begin, and along the ridge to the cardamom country around Thekkady. One long descent takes you down to the backwaters for a night on a houseboat and two nights on the lake at Kumarakom. The last night is by the sea at Kovalam.

We've left more of this journey unscheduled than usual. In Kerala, an afternoon by the pool watching a kingfisher on the fence is time well spent.

## Who this journey suits

Second-time visitors to India who did the north last time. Honeymooners who want more than a beach. Anyone who cares about food, since you eat differently every day here: Syrian Christian beef fry in Kochi, a hill-station breakfast of puttu and kadala in Munnar, fresh karimeen on the boat, a 24-dish sadya in the village. Families with children of any age. Travellers who want an introduction to India with shorter drives than the north.

## How we adjust it

Many guests add a week of Ayurveda at a retreat near Kollam, and we fold it in after the backwaters. If you prefer a quieter, more bohemian beach, swap Kovalam for the cliffs at Varkala. A night at Marari puts you on the sand straight after the houseboat. And if you'd rather see wildlife than tea, we trade the second Munnar night for a night in Wayanad or at a tiger reserve.
