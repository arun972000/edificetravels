This is the journey we suggest when a family tells us they want India but aren't sure a week of monuments will hold a twelve-year-old. It doesn't try to. Instead it alternates: a train ride and three days of jeep safaris, then a city of forts and bazaars where you make things with your hands, then three slow nights by a lake with a palace in the middle of it.

Ranthambore is the heart of it. Most of India's tiger parks are deep forest where the cats are hard to see. Ranthambore is dry, open woodland around three lakes and a hilltop fort, and its tigers have grown up with jeeps. Five drives over three days is the right number: enough for a good chance of a sighting, and enough to stop worrying about the sighting and start noticing the owls, the crocodiles and the langurs stealing from the temple.

Then Jaipur, which we do at a child's pace: forts by jeep, a puppet-maker's home, an afternoon printing your own cloth. And Udaipur to finish, where the pace drops right off and the only decision is whether to climb a hill fort or stay by the pool.

## Who this journey suits

Families with children from about six, who want wildlife and castles in the same trip. Couples who want tigers without a long wilderness circuit. First-time visitors who'd like a shorter, gentler Rajasthan with fewer long drives than the full western route. Grandparents travelling with grandchildren do very well on it, since the hotels have pools and the days have breaks.

## How we adjust it

Give us two more days and we add Agra and the Taj between Delhi and Ranthambore, by road. For more tiger time, add a third park day with two more safaris, or a night at Bandhavgarh on a longer route. The Udaipur hill-fort day can become a cookery class or a half-day at a rural school project. Older children and teenagers often prefer Jodhpur's zip line over Mehrangarh to a third Udaipur night, and we can route for that.
