The Indian Himalaya runs for more than two thousand kilometres, from the high desert of Ladakh to the tea gardens below Kanchenjunga, and it's really several different mountain worlds. Ladakh is Buddhist, dry and very high, a place of monasteries and lakes behind the main range. Kashmir is green and Mughal, with lakes and meadows. Himachal and Uttarakhand are the hill towns and ashrams of the foothills. Darjeeling, in the east, is tea, toy trains and the third-highest mountain on earth.

None of them needs you to be a mountaineer. Most of our Himalayan journeys are travelled by car, with walks as long or short as you want and nights in good hotels, family guesthouses or village homestays. What they do need is respect for the altitude, the season and the weather, which is where planning makes the difference.

## How we plan a mountain journey

Altitude first. Above about 3,000 metres we build in two quiet days before anything strenuous and climb gradually after that. Then the season, which decides almost everything: Ladakh only from June to September, the lower hills in spring and autumn. Then a spare day, because flights wait for the wind and roads close for snow and landslides.

We use local drivers who know their roads, and homestays where they're the best way to see a valley. If you want to walk further, we'll plan a trek with ponies and a cook that suits your fitness.

## Who it suits

Travellers who have seen the plains and want something bigger, photographers, keen walkers, and anyone who finds the heat of the plains too much. Kashmir and the hill towns suit families and older travellers as well; Ladakh suits anyone in reasonable health who takes the altitude seriously.
