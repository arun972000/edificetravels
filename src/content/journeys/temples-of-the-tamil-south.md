The Mughals barely reached the Tamil south, and it shows. The great buildings here are temples still in daily use, each one a small walled city: gateway towers painted in every colour, an elephant at the door, and priests keeping the same rituals at the same hours they have for centuries. The food changes, and so do the script and the music. Travellers who know the north often say it feels like a second country, and in most ways it is.

The route runs down the coast and then inland. A night in Chennai to land, then the rock-cut monuments and the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram. Two nights in Pondicherry, where France left its grid of yellow streets and the croissants are still good. Then the Chola heartland around Thanjavur, with the finest stone carving in India, and a night in a Chettinad merchant's mansion with a cooking lesson in its kitchen. Madurai, the great temple city, gets two nights so you can see the night ceremony. After that you climb over the Western Ghats into Kerala for a boat on Periyar Lake and a last night in Fort Kochi.

The guides matter more here than anywhere. A Tamil temple without someone to read it is a wall of stone gods; with the right person it is a story that takes a morning to tell.

## Who this journey suits

Second or third visits to India, when the Golden Triangle is done and you want depth. Travellers with an interest in architecture, religion or sculpture. Anyone who cares about food: the south is India's great vegetarian kitchen, and Chettinad is its boldest. Older travellers who prefer good roads and gentle walking to long treks, since there's almost no climbing on this route. It suits couples and solo travellers better than young children, who may find the number of temples hard going.

## How we adjust it

Add two days and we fit in Tiruchirappalli's Rock Fort and the Srirangam temple between Thanjavur and Chettinad. Add Kerala's backwaters and tea hills at the end to make a fortnight. If the city doesn't interest you, skip Chennai and go straight to Mahabalipuram, an hour from the airport. And if you'd rather see textiles than temples, we add Kanchipuram's silk weavers on day two and a kalamkari workshop near Pondicherry.
