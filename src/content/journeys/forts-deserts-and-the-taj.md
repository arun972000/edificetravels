This is the India most people picture before they come, and we've spent years taking the clichés out of it. You see the Taj twice: at dusk from Mehtab Bagh, the Mughal garden directly across the river, and at dawn from inside the gates, before the crowds. You go up the ramparts of Amber by jeep before the buses arrive, and you walk Mehrangarh with a historian who played in its courtyards as a boy.

The twelve days work because of the direction. You travel in one long arc, Delhi to Udaipur, and never double back. Heading west, the land gets drier and redder, the food hotter, the turbans brighter. Two nights in the desert outside Jaisalmer come next. After them, Udaipur's white palaces and its lake feel like a reward, which is why we put them last.

## Who this journey suits

First-time visitors who want the classics done properly. Couples celebrating something. Solo travellers who want structure and safety without joining a group. Families with children over about ten, who love forts and camels and will put up with two long drives to get to them. It isn't for anyone who wants to be on a beach by day four, or who needs every day to be a short one.

## How we adjust it

Most guests change something. Add three days and we put Ranthambore's tigers between Agra and Jaipur. If a tent isn't your idea of a good night, swap the desert camp for a second night in Udaipur. Fly from Jaisalmer to save a day on the road, or drive it for the marble temple at Ranakpur. Your specialist will rework the plan around whatever matters most to you.
