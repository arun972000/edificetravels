Kashmir is the valley Mughal emperors came to every summer to escape the heat of the plains, and the reasons are still obvious from a shikara on Dal Lake: a ring of mountains, still water, willow and poplar along the shore, and the gardens they built on the slopes above. It's green when the rest of north India is brown, and cool when Delhi passes forty degrees.

Srinagar is the place to stay. The British, barred from owning land, built houseboats instead, and their descendants still line Dal and Nigeen lakes: long cedar boats with carved walnut ceilings, a sitting room, a dining room and two or three bedrooms, run by the same families for generations. One or two nights aboard is the classic way to start. Dal is the busier lake, close to the boulevard and the gardens. Nigeen, to the north, is quieter, and it's where we send people who want to hear the water at night. After that we move you to a hotel on the shore for the rest of your time in the city.

From Srinagar the valley opens out. Gulmarg, two hours west, is a meadow at 2,650 metres ringed by pine forest, with a gondola climbing towards Apharwat peak. In winter it's India's main ski resort. Pahalgam, two and a half hours east, sits where two rivers meet below the pine-covered hills, with the Aru valley a short drive further up, and it's the base for gentle walks along the Lidder. Sonamarg, the meadow of gold, is on the road towards Ladakh and the Zoji La.

We plan Kashmir for April to June and September to October. April brings the tulip garden in Srinagar and blossom in the orchards. May and June are the green months, and September and October are clear and cool, with the chinars turning red. July and August are the season of the Amarnath pilgrimage, which passes through Pahalgam, and roads can close for convoys.
