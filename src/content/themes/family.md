India is one of the most rewarding places in the world to bring children, and one of the most tiring if the trip is planned for adults. The answer is a slower pace, shorter drives and a few things on every day that are just for them. Get that right and children tend to love it: the forts look like the castles in their books, the animals are everywhere, and almost everyone they meet is delighted to see them.

Rajasthan is the classic family route. Jaipur has a fort you go up by jeep and a palace full of armour; Ranthambore has tigers; Jaisalmer has camels and a fort that people still live in; Udaipur has a lake and boats. In the south, Kerala gives you a night on a houseboat, elephants at Periyar and a beach at the end, at a much gentler pace.

## How we plan a family journey

We ask the children's ages first and plan from there. Drives stay short, with a stop every two hours. Early starts are for the things that need them, and afternoons are for swimming and rest. We choose hotels with pools, gardens and family rooms, and we tell you honestly which grand hotels are better without small children.

The same driver stays with you throughout, and your specialist is on WhatsApp for the small emergencies: a forgotten favourite toy, a stomach that needs plain rice, a day that needs changing.

## Who it suits

Families with children from about six to sixteen, multi-generational groups, and families from the diaspora showing children where their grandparents came from. It works best between October and March, outside the hottest months.
