## Executive Summary

Edifice Travels is a small New Delhi company that designs private journeys through India for travellers from abroad. The voice is that of a well-travelled friend who lives in India: someone who knows which gate opens first at the Taj, which driver to put with a nervous first-timer, and which lunch is worth a two-hour drive. It is warm without gushing, exact without being dry, and honest about what is hard.

The core idea is architectural. A jharokha is the carved window of an Indian palace, the place from which you watched the city below. Edifice means a building. The brand opens a window onto India for people who cannot yet see it from where they are, and the person who opens it lives here. Every piece of writing should feel like someone standing beside you at that window, pointing things out.

What makes the voice distinctive in this market is specificity. Competitors describe India with adjectives (vibrant, breathtaking, magical). Edifice describes it with facts a guest can use: times, distances, names of dishes, the order in which to do things. The writing earns emotion through detail, never through superlatives.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Local** — we live where you are going and write from experience | **A booking desk** — anonymous, abroad, reading from a supplier list |
| **Specific** — times, distances, names, the order to do things | **Adjectival** — vibrant, breathtaking, magical, hidden gem |
| **Warm** — a friend's voice, contractions, small human details | **Gushing** — exclamation marks, "unforgettable", sales pressure |
| **Honest** — we say what is hard, crowded, hot or tiring | **Brochure-perfect** — only sunsets, never the queue |
| **Calm and assured** — we have done this many times | **Pushy** — urgency, discounts, countdowns, "limited spots" |
| **Plain-spoken** — short sentences, everyday words | **Ornate** — flowery metaphors, long chains of clauses |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Local
- **What it means**: The writer lives in India and has done the thing being described.
- **How it shows up**: "a historian who grew up beneath it" (Forts, Deserts and the Taj); "a stall that has fried them since 1875" (The Grand Tour).
- **What to avoid**: Generic third-person description that could be written from anywhere.
- **Evidence**: Journey copy in src/content/journeys; planner positioning ("people who live here").
- **Confidence**: High

#### Specific
- **What it means**: Replace every adjective you can with a fact.
- **How it shows up**: Drive times on every day, opening hours, hotel names by style, "the gates open 30 minutes before sunrise".
- **What to avoid**: "Stunning views", "rich culture", "a feast for the senses".
- **Evidence**: e-Visa guide (fees, timings, step by step); competitor audit (adjective-led rivals).
- **Confidence**: High

#### Warm
- **What it means**: Write the way a friend talks, not the way a company announces.
- **How it shows up**: Contractions, second person, small human moments ("you will already be at breakfast").
- **What to avoid**: Exclamation marks, forced enthusiasm, emojis.
- **Evidence**: Home copy in en.ts ("A day in India"), journey FAQs.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### Honest
- **What it means**: Name the difficulties so guests trust the rest.
- **How it shows up** (illustrative): naming the long drive days, "We advise against May to August" (Forts, Deserts and the Taj FAQ), saying plainly when a wildlife sighting is unlikely.
- **What to avoid**: Pretending every day is easy or every hotel is perfect.
- **Evidence**: Journey FAQs, destination tips, planner trust architecture.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Calm and assured
- **What it means**: Confidence comes from experience, so it never needs to shout.
- **How it shows up**: the 48-hour quote promise stated once, plainly; "we re-route around it at no extra cost where we can" (how-booking-works page).
- **What to avoid**: Countdown timers, "book now", "only 2 left".
- **Evidence**: Planner, section 5.4 (patterns to avoid); src/pages/how-booking-works.astro.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### Plain-spoken
- **What it means**: Short sentences, common words, one idea at a time.
- **How it shows up**: Sentence-case headings, buttons that say exactly what happens ("Send my plan").
- **What to avoid**: Jargon ("curated bespoke experiences"), stacked clauses.
- **Evidence**: en.ts microcopy, design direction principles.
- **Confidence**: High

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
A private India journey designed by someone who lives here, quoted in writing within 48 hours, with one person on WhatsApp from your first message to your flight home.

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Someone who lives here**
   - Core idea: the company is a small team in India; each guest has one specialist who knows the ground first-hand.
   - When to use: hero, about page, specialist cards.
   - Example phrasing: "A journey designed by someone who lives here."

2. **One person, start to finish**
   - Core idea: no hand-offs; the planner is the person who answers while you travel.
   - When to use: enquiry points, how booking works, reviews.
   - Example phrasing: "The person who designs your journey is the one on WhatsApp while you travel."

3. **A quote you can read**
   - Core idea: prices are private, but every quote is itemised, with hotels named and nothing hidden.
   - When to use: wherever a price would otherwise appear.
   - Example phrasing: "Your itemised quote, with every hotel named, arrives within 48 hours."

4. **India as it really is**
   - Core idea: we tell you what is hard as plainly as what is beautiful.
   - When to use: FAQs, guides, destination tips.
   - Example phrasing: "It is loud and crowded, and it will change you."

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. foreign-owned specialists (Audley, Enchanting Travels): we are on the ground, not in London or New York; no overseas intermediary.
- vs. India-based catalogues (Cholan Tours, Tour My India): one focused inbound brand, real itinerary detail, a named person, a reply-time promise.
- vs. OTAs and marketplaces (MakeMyTrip, TravelTriangle): no discount theatre, no agent lottery, no anonymous packages.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Formality | Energy | Detail | Key principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home page and heroes | Medium | Medium-high | Low | One clear promise, one emotional image |
| Journey pages | Medium | Medium | High | Every day useful: time, place, drive time, where you stay |
| Travel guides | Medium | Calm | High | Answer the question in the first two sentences |
| Planner and forms | Medium-low | Calm | Low | Say exactly what happens next |
| WhatsApp replies | Low | Warm | Medium | Use the guest's name; answer first, then ask |
| Quote emails | Medium-high | Calm | High | Itemised, named, dated, no surprises |
| Social media | Low | Medium-high | Low | One real moment, one photograph, no hashtag walls |
| Errors and empty states | Medium | Neutral | Low | What went wrong and how to fix it; no apology |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Home page and heroes
- **Do**: lead with the feeling, prove it in the next sentence with a fact.
- **Don't**: stack three adjectives; highlight one word in a headline.
- **Example**: "Some places you see. India, you feel."

#### Journey pages
- **Do**: open each day with what happens, include the drive time, name the hotel by style.
- **Don't**: use "explore", "discover" or "immerse" (banned everywhere; see Terminology).
- **Example**: "Inside the gates as they open, when the marble is pink and the crowds are still in bed."

#### WhatsApp
- **Do**: reply in the guest's terms, keep messages short, confirm the next step and time.
- **Don't**: send a wall of text or a sales script.
- **Example**: "Hello [first name], thanks for this. February is a lovely time for Rajasthan. Two quick questions so I can draw a first route for you tomorrow."

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| journey | Our itineraries | tour, package, trip product |
| specialist | The person who designs and supports a journey | agent, consultant, executive |
| quote | The written, itemised price sent after enquiry | price list, package price |
| style (Comfort, Premium, Luxury) | How a journey can be stayed | tier, category, class |
| driver | The person who drives you throughout | chauffeur (use "chauffeur" only in legal or insurance wording) |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| package | Sounds like a mass-market product | journey |
| bespoke, curated | Overused luxury jargon | designed, chosen, tailor-made |
| explore, discover, immerse | Empty verbs (banned everywhere) | the actual action: walk, eat, watch, drive |
| hidden gem | Cliché, and rarely true | name the place and why |
| vibrant, breathtaking, magical, mesmerising, nestled, bustling, rich tapestry, plethora, paradise, unforgettable, bucket list, must-see | Travel clichés | a specific fact |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|---|---|
| "best price", "cheapest", "limited offer" | Prices are private; we do not compete on discounts |
| "we reply in [language]" | Not offered until multilingual staff is confirmed |
| Ministry of Tourism, IATO or other badges | Not held yet |

## Humanisation Rules (no generated-text tells)

These apply to every word on the site. The English text is the voice reference; translations follow the same rules, checked by a native speaker (see open questions).

1. **No em dashes or spaced en dashes in sentences.** Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. Ranges use "to" ("October to March"), not a dash.
2. **No middle-dot strings** ("Delhi · Agra · Jaipur"). Use commas, or "Delhi to Udaipur".
3. **No arrows or symbols in copy** (→, ↗, ✦, ★ as decoration). Links and buttons say what they do.
4. **No "not X, but Y"** or "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions.
5. **No padding in threes.** Lists of three adjectives or three parallel clauses only when there really are three things.
6. **No highlighted single word** in headlines (italic, colour or bold).
7. **No all-caps labels** and no labels above headings unless they add information.
8. **No rhetorical openers**: "Imagine...", "Picture this...", "Whether you're...", "Welcome to...".
9. **No filler closers**: "And so much more", "The possibilities are endless", "Your adventure awaits".
10. **Vary sentence length.** Mix short sentences with longer ones; avoid every paragraph having the same rhythm.
11. **Prefer the specific noun.** "Laal maas" not "local delicacies"; "the 6 am gate" not "early access".
12. **Use contractions where a person would** ("you'll", "we'd"), but not in legal pages.
13. **Exclamation marks: none.**
14. **Numbers as people say them**: "twenty minutes later", "a two-hour drive", digits for times, prices and dates.

## Content Examples

### Excellent
> "Inside the gates as they open, when the marble is pink and the crowds are still in bed. Breakfast back at the hotel, then the ghost city of Fatehpur Sikri on the road west."

Why it works: a time, a colour, a place, a sequence. No adjective is doing work that a fact could do.

### To avoid
> "Immerse yourself in the breathtaking beauty of the iconic Taj Mahal — a must-see wonder that will leave you speechless!"

What is wrong: cliché verb, two clichés, an em dash, an exclamation mark, and no fact a guest can use. Better: "The Taj at first light, before the gates fill. Twenty minutes later the crowds arrive; you'll already be at breakfast."

## Open Questions for Team Discussion

### High Priority
1. **Can we keep the 12-hour reply and 48-hour quote promises?**
   - What was found: both promises appear on every enquiry point, the journey pages and the booking terms, but no source confirms the team can meet them in peak season and across US and UK evenings.
   - Agent recommendation: confirm a rota and a quote template before launch; if peak-season capacity is uncertain, change the quote promise to "within two working days" rather than miss it.
   - Need from you: confirmation of both timings, or the timings you can guarantee.

2. **Which languages does the voice cover?**
   - What was found: the site is translated into German, French, Spanish and Italian, while replies are offered in English only.
   - Agent recommendation: keep English as the voice reference; have one native speaker per language apply these rules to the translations before launch; add "we reply in Deutsch" and similar only once staff are in place.
   - Need from you: native-speaker reviewers, and a decision on multilingual replies.

3. **Founder's voice**
   - What was found: the home letter is signed "The team at Edifice Travels"; no founder name is confirmed.
   - Agent recommendation: sign it with the founder's real name and add a photo; it becomes the most trusted paragraph on the site.
   - Need from you: the founder's name, a two-line story and permission to use it.

4. **Real WhatsApp tone**
   - What was found: no real guest conversations were available.
   - Agent recommendation: share five anonymised WhatsApp threads that went well, and we'll refine the WhatsApp and quote-email tone from them.
   - Need from you: the threads, with names removed.

### Medium Priority
5. **Signature experiences**
   - What was found: family-kitchen dinners and Bishnoi village visits appear in itineraries, but nothing is named as uniquely yours.
   - Agent recommendation: choose three experiences only Edifice offers and name them consistently everywhere.
   - Need from you: the three experiences.

### Low Priority
6. **British or American spelling**
   - What was found: the site uses British spelling (travellers, colour), which suits a global audience.
   - Agent recommendation: keep British spelling sitewide.
   - Need from you: confirm.
