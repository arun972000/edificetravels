The Indian Himalaya is really two regions, and which one you visit depends on when you travel. South of the main range are the foothills: Rishikesh on the Ganges, Shimla and Dharamshala in Himachal, and Amritsar on the Punjab plain below them. They're green and forested, between 300 and 2,200 metres, and best from October to April, when the air is clear and the snow line sits close above you. In July and August they take the full force of the monsoon, and the roads slide.

North of the range are Ladakh and Spiti, high, dry and Buddhist, mostly above 3,500 metres. Snow cuts them off from November to May, and their short summer runs from June to September, exactly when the rest of India is wet. The passes that lead in are among the highest roads in the world. You can't do both halves properly in one trip, so we plan two kinds of Himalayan journey and tell you plainly which one suits the month you can travel.

The foothills journey begins in Amritsar with the Golden Temple, climbs through the Kangra valley to Dharamshala, then runs east along the Himachal hills to Shimla, with the toy train down to the plains at the end. It can also begin in Rishikesh, with a week of yoga by the river. The high journey flies into Leh, rests, and then moves slowly through the monasteries and lakes of Ladakh, with the option of the long road south over the passes to Manali.

## How long you need

A week in the foothills covers Amritsar, Dharamshala and Shimla, with a night in the Kangra valley in between. Ten days adds Rishikesh at the end, by road through Dehradun. Ladakh needs seven nights at the very least, including two to acclimatise, and ten to reach Pangong, Nubra and the Sham valley without rushing. Spiti is a two-week road journey from Shimla to Manali, for people who are fit and in no hurry. Any of these combines well with a few days in Delhi or Rajasthan at either end.
