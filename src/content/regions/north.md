Most journeys to India begin in the north, and much of the country's history happened here. There are the Sultanate and Mughal capitals of Delhi, the Taj Mahal at Agra, and Varanasi, where Hindus have worshipped on the same riverbank for three thousand years. This is also the India of the great plain, flat, fertile and crowded, where the cities are big and far enough apart that you need a plan.

The classic route is Delhi, Agra and Varanasi, and it works. Spend two nights in Delhi to find your feet and see the Mughal and Sultanate monuments, then a night in Agra for the Taj at dawn, the Fort and Fatehpur Sikri. From Agra you fly to Varanasi for two nights on the river, because the road takes nine hours and the train six. Flights from Varanasi go to Delhi, Khajuraho and Kolkata, so the journey can turn in any direction from there.

We'd rather slow it down and add the places in between. Orchha, on the Betwa river three hours south of Agra, has sixteenth-century palaces and riverside cenotaphs and almost no visitors. Khajuraho, three hours further on, has some of the finest temple carving in India. Lucknow, between Agra and Varanasi, is known for its kebabs and for a courtly politeness that has outlasted the Nawabs. Each one adds a day, and each gives you a night somewhere quieter between the big monuments.

## How long you need

Five nights covers Delhi, Agra and Varanasi, with a flight between the last two. Eight lets you add Orchha and Khajuraho by road and fly from Khajuraho to Varanasi. Ten gives you Lucknow as well, or two extra nights in Delhi for the parts of the city most visitors miss. Most of our guests combine the north with Rajasthan in a fortnight, going from Delhi to Agra, then Jaipur and further west, and come back for Varanasi on a second trip. If you have two weeks for the north alone, ending in Varanasi, that's the journey we'd most like to plan for you.
