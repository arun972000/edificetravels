The Ganges comes out of the Himalaya at Rishikesh. Above the town it's a mountain river, green and fast and cold. Below it the plains begin, and the river slows and spreads out towards Haridwar. Hindus have come to this gap in the hills to meditate for as long as there are records, and the ashrams along the river have been teaching yoga to Westerners since 1968, when the Beatles spent several weeks at the Maharishi Mahesh Yogi's compound and wrote much of the White Album there. That ashram is still here, abandoned and covered in murals, inside a tiger reserve.

The town has two halves. Downstream, around Triveni Ghat, is the Indian pilgrim town with its temples, sweet shops and evening aarti. Upstream, across the Ram Jhula suspension bridge, are the ashrams of Swargashram, and beyond them the cafés and yoga schools of Tapovan and Laxman Jhula, where the backpackers stay. We usually put you above both, in the quiet resorts along the river towards Shivpuri, and bring you down for the evening ceremony and the morning classes. If you want to stay in an ashram we can arrange it, but know what it involves first: early mornings, simple food and no coffee.

Give it two or three nights. Spend a morning on yoga and a walk across the bridges, and another rafting or driving up to Kunjapuri at dawn, with an evening at the aarti in between. For guests who want longer, we design week-long stays that combine yoga, Ayurveda and the river. Rishikesh is also the start of the roads into the high Garhwal Himalaya and the Char Dham pilgrimage, and it's forty minutes from Dehradun's airport, which makes it the gentlest way we know to begin a mountain journey.
