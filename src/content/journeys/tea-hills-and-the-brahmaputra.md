Almost nobody from abroad goes east. They should. Kolkata is the best-fed city in India and the most argumentative, and peepal trees are slowly taking back its colonial centre. Darjeeling has Kanchenjunga, 8,586 metres, turning pink at dawn above the tea gardens, and a Victorian steam railway still running up the hill to it. And Kaziranga, on the Brahmaputra floodplain in Assam, is the one Indian park where we can promise you the animal on the poster. It has two thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos, and they graze beside the track.

The route goes north, then east. Two nights in Kolkata, mostly on foot and mostly eating. A flight to the foothills and three nights in Darjeeling, with a dawn at Tiger Hill, a steam train and a day on a tea estate. Then one long travel day to Assam, three nights at Kaziranga with five safaris, and a last night in Guwahati for the Kamakhya temple and the river at sunset.

Food gets as much attention here as wildlife: mustard fish in Kolkata, Tibetan momos in the hills, and an Assamese thali of a dozen small sour and smoky dishes. You'll eat something different every day.

## Who this journey suits

Travellers who have done Rajasthan and the Taj and want a part of the country that isn't on the usual circuit. Wildlife lovers who'd rather have a guaranteed big animal than a tiger lottery. Food people. Tea drinkers, obviously. Anyone drawn to colonial history and hill stations. Reasonably fit adults of any age: the only early starts are Tiger Hill and the safaris, and the only long day is day six. Teenagers enjoy it. Younger children may find the travel day hard.

## How we adjust it

Add two nights and we take you from Guwahati to Majuli, the largest river island in India, with its Vaishnavite monasteries and mask-makers. Add three and we go south to Meghalaya for the living root bridges of Cherrapunji. In any style, you can swap one Darjeeling night for a night on a tea estate at Glenburn or Makaibari. If you'd rather not fly twice, we route Kolkata to Darjeeling by the overnight train to New Jalpaiguri, which some guests prefer anyway.
