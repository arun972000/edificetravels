Jaipur was planned, which is rare in India, and the plan still works. Nine rectangular blocks sit inside pink walls, with a street for every trade and a palace at the centre where the royal family still lives. The city has never stopped making things either: cut gems, block-printed cottons, blue pottery, enamelled jewellery, and the printed quilts you'll find in shops across Europe.

Most visitors arrive from Agra or Delhi, and this is where India starts to feel like Rajasthan. The turbans get brighter and the food gets hotter. Amber, eleven kilometres out of town, is the great one, a hill palace of courtyards and mirrored halls that took over a century to build. We go early, by jeep, and leave as the crowds come in.

Then the city itself. The Hawa Mahal is the honeycomb façade built so the women of the court could watch the street without being seen. At Jantar Mantar an astronomer king built stone instruments the size of houses to track the sun and stars, and the great sundial still keeps time to within two seconds. The City Palace has painted gates and the two largest silver vessels in the world, urns a maharaja filled with Ganges water for a trip to England. Around all of it are the bazaars, where you'll spend more time than you planned. That's why we suggest a third night.
