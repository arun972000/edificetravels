The Golden Triangle is where most people start, and for good reason: the Taj, the forts and the Mughal capital are about as good as monuments get. Delhi, Agra and Jaipur show you what emperors and maharajas built. Varanasi shows you how people live and die, on the same river steps, every day. It's the part guests are still talking about a year later.

The nine days run in a clean line. Two nights in Delhi to shake off the flight and see both the Mughal city and the British one. One night in Agra, timed so you see the Taj twice: across the river at dusk and inside the gates at dawn. Two nights in Jaipur, with Amber Fort by jeep and dinner in a family's kitchen. Then a 90-minute flight east to the Ganges, two nights on the river, and a short flight back to Delhi for your departure.

Varanasi is the reason we don't rush this. You need two evenings and one dawn on the water, with a guide who knows which ghat does what and when to look away. We use hotels on or beside the river, so the temple bells are the last thing you hear at night.

## Who this journey suits

First-time visitors who want the classics and one thing beyond them. Travellers with an interest in religion, history or photography, who'll get more from Varanasi than most. Couples and solo travellers. It's one of our most-booked routes for solo women, because the driver and guides are long-serving and the hotels are chosen for their location. Teenagers tend to be gripped by Varanasi. Children under ten may find it a lot.

## How we adjust it

Add two days and we put Ranthambore between Agra and Jaipur for tigers. Add one and you get Khajuraho's temples on the way to Varanasi, by road and train. For more time on the river, we take the second Delhi night and give it to Varanasi, with a day trip to the weavers' villages. And if you'd rather make something than cook it, swap the Jaipur family dinner for a block-printing workshop at Bagru.
