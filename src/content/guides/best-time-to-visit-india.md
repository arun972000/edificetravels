India is roughly the size of Western Europe and runs from Himalayan passes to a tropical coast, so no single month suits all of it. There is a clear best time for the trip most first-time visitors want, though, and there are good reasons to come at other times too. We plan journeys in every month of the year.

## The short answer

For a journey through Delhi, Agra, Rajasthan and Kerala, go between October and March. November to February is the peak, with dry, sunny days of 20 to 28°C across the plains and cool nights. About 43% of all foreign arrivals land in those four months, so the best hotels fill early and prices are at their highest. October and March give you nearly the same weather for less money, with fewer people around.

## Month by month

- **January:** Cool and dry everywhere south of the Himalaya. Delhi mornings can be foggy and 7°C, which delays flights. Rajasthan is perfect and Kerala is warm and dry. Peak prices.
- **February:** The best all-round month. A little warmer, with clear skies and wildlife parks in good shape. Still peak pricing, but the fog has gone.
- **March:** Warm days around 30°C in the north, hot by the end of the month. Holi falls in March. Rates begin to drop after the middle of the month.
- **April:** Hot on the plains, 35 to 40°C. Good for the Himalayan foothills. Wildlife sightings improve as animals come to water.
- **May:** The hottest month across most of India. Go to the hill stations, Ladakh or high Himachal, and nowhere else.
- **June:** The monsoon reaches Kerala in the first week and moves north through the month. Ladakh is dry and open.
- **July and August:** Monsoon across most of the country, with green countryside and heavy humidity. It's Kerala's Ayurveda season and Ladakh's best weather, while Rajasthan is hot and intermittently wet.
- **September:** The monsoon retreats. The Northeast and the hills clear, Rajasthan is green and empty, and rates are low.
- **October:** The plains dry out and the season opens. Expect warm afternoons, good light and Diwali celebrations. The first half of the month is excellent value.
- **November:** Peak season begins. The Pushkar camel fair, cool evenings, and every region open except the high Himalaya.
- **December:** Cold nights in Rajasthan, down to 5°C in the desert. Hornbill in Nagaland. Many hotels add Christmas and New Year supplements.

## Which region in which month?

Most of India is at its best between October and March. From June to September the main exceptions are Ladakh, which is at its best, and Kerala, where the monsoon brings the Ayurveda season.

| Region | Oct to Nov | Dec to Feb | Mar to May | Jun to Sep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delhi, Agra and Rajasthan | Excellent | Excellent, cold nights | Hot from April | Monsoon, humid |
| Varanasi and the Ganges plain | Good | Excellent, foggy mornings | Very hot | Monsoon, high river |
| Kerala and Tamil Nadu | Good, some late rain | Excellent | Hot and humid | Monsoon, Ayurveda season |
| Goa | Good from late October | Excellent | Hot, quieter | Monsoon, mostly closed |
| Ladakh | Closing, cold | Closed to most travel | Opens from May | Excellent |
| Himachal and Uttarakhand hills | Excellent | Snow at altitude | Excellent | Monsoon, landslide risk |
| Northeast (Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland) | Excellent | Excellent, Hornbill | Good until April | Heavy monsoon |
| Wildlife parks (Ranthambore, Kanha, Bandhavgarh) | Open, lush | Good | Best sightings, hot | Most parks closed |

## Which festivals are worth planning around?

Five are worth building a trip around: Diwali, Holi, the Pushkar camel fair, Onam and Nagaland's Hornbill Festival. All except Hornbill follow the lunar calendar and move each year, so we confirm the dates when we build your itinerary.

**Diwali** (late October or November; 8 November in 2026). The festival of lights is celebrated everywhere. Cities stay lit for a week, families exchange sweets and the fireworks go on late. Some monuments and offices close on the day itself, and hotels fill. Jaipur and Udaipur are good places to spend it.

**Holi** (March; 22 March in 2027). The festival of colour is mostly a northern celebration. Joining in is great fun, but street Holi can get rough, particularly for women, so we arrange it at a hotel or haveli where the hosts play with their guests. Rajasthan and Mathura are the heart of it.

**Pushkar camel fair** (November; the week leading up to 24 November in 2026). Tens of thousands of camels, horses and traders gather in the desert town of Pushkar. Hotel prices rise steeply and the tents sell out, so book months ahead.

**Onam** (August or September; late August in 2026). Kerala's harvest festival brings flower carpets, snake boat races and the sadya feast on a banana leaf. It falls during the monsoon, which keeps the crowds mostly Indian and makes it the best cultural reason to visit Kerala in the rains.

**Hornbill Festival** (1 to 10 December, every year). Nagaland's tribes gather at Kisama, near Kohima, for ten days of dance, music and food. It's the only festival on this list with fixed dates. Combine it with Kaziranga and Meghalaya.

## Where does shoulder season pay off?

Most of all in October and March, when you get close to peak weather at lower rates. Kerala in the monsoon is worth it if you're coming for Ayurveda, and Ladakh has a short season of its own.

**October in Rajasthan.** Afternoons reach 33°C, but mornings and evenings are pleasant, hotels haven't reached peak rates and the countryside is still green after the rains. It's our favourite month for value in the north.

**March in the north and the south.** Warm until the last week, when the heat builds. Wildlife is easy to see, Holi falls this month and prices are coming down.

**Kerala in the monsoon.** Ayurvedic physicians consider the cool, damp monsoon months the best time for treatment, because the body is receptive and the dust has gone. Resorts drop their rates and the backwaters are at their greenest. Come for a two- or three-week programme. It's the wrong season for a beach holiday.

**Ladakh from June to September.** This is the only time most of the region is open. It sits behind the main Himalayan range, so the monsoon largely passes it by.

## What to avoid

The plains from mid-April to June, when 40°C is routine and sightseeing becomes an endurance test. Goa and the Himalayan foothills in July and August, when rain disrupts the roads. And, unless you're happy to pay for it, the fortnight around Christmas and New Year, when the best hotels charge their highest rates and some add compulsory gala dinners.
