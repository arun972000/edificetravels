After the forts and the desert, Udaipur is a long breath out. Maharana Udai Singh II founded the city in 1559 as the Mughals closed in on Chittorgarh, and he chose the site for its water: a chain of lakes in a bowl of the Aravalli hills, far enough from the plains to be safe. The Mewar dynasty that built it claims the longest unbroken line of any ruling family in the world, and its descendants still live in the City Palace, which runs for nearly 250 metres along the eastern shore of Lake Pichola.

That shore is the reason to come. The palace is a huge white and cream cliff of balconies and cupolas. Seen from a boat at sunset, with the Lake Palace on the water in front of it and Jag Mandir behind, it's the view most people picture when they think of Rajasthan. We do the boat on your first evening so you have the shape of the city before you walk it. The next morning is for the palace itself, the Jagdish temple at its gate and the lanes of miniature painters below, followed by a long lunch somewhere with a view.

Udaipur is also the best base for the hills. Kumbhalgarh fort and the Ranakpur Jain temple are a day trip to the north, through forest and farming villages where the women wear mirror-work skirts to work in the fields. For the last sunset we send you up to the Monsoon Palace on its hill above the city. Two nights is enough. Three lets you stop, swim and do nothing for an afternoon, which after ten days of Rajasthan is what most people need. There are daily flights to Delhi and Mumbai, so the journey can end here or carry on south.
