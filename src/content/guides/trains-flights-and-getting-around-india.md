Distances in India deceive. Two hundred and fifty kilometres can be three hours on an expressway or seven on a two-lane road shared with trucks, tractors and a wedding procession. We plan each leg separately, choosing between your own car and driver, a domestic flight and a train.

## Why is a private car and driver the backbone?

Because it goes door to door, stops where you want and carries your bags, and no train or flight does all three. The drive from Agra to Jaipur becomes part of the sightseeing when it takes in Fatehpur Sikri, the abandoned Mughal city, and the Abhaneri stepwell. Jodhpur to Udaipur is a plain transfer by air, and a full day out by road when it passes the Ranakpur temples and the Aravalli hills.

You keep one driver for the whole journey. He knows the hotels, the clean restaurants on the highway and where the checkpoints are, and by day three he knows how you like your mornings. For two to four people the car is a Toyota Innova Crysta or equivalent, air-conditioned, with room for everyone's luggage. Larger groups travel in a Tempo Traveller. Tourist vehicles carry a yellow commercial plate and an All India Tourist Permit. Drivers sleep in the driver accommodation Indian hotels provide, which is why your quote includes their lodging.

Plan around two limits. A driving day shouldn't go beyond eight hours, and we don't run highway drives after dark, because the roads are unlit and the trucks don't stop. And don't drive yourself. Traffic rules are loosely observed and any scrape turns into a difficult negotiation.

## When should you fly instead?

Fly when the drive would take more than eight or nine hours. Under six hours, we drive; in between, we look for a fast train. Some sectors are always flights: Udaipur to Delhi or Mumbai, Delhi to Varanasi, Delhi to Leh, and anything to Kerala, Goa, Kolkata or the Northeast.

IndiGo, Air India and Akasa cover every route you'll need. Book three to six weeks ahead for the best fares, because last-minute tickets in peak season can cost double. Most domestic economy fares include 15 kg of checked baggage and 7 kg in the cabin, which is tight for a three-week trip, so buy extra weight when you book. It costs more at the counter. Arrive two hours before departure. Delhi has three terminals a long way apart and IndiGo uses more than one, so check your terminal the night before. Your driver will know.

## Which trains are worth taking?

The fast day trains. Vande Bharat and Shatabdi expresses run at up to 130 to 160 km/h between major cities, in air-conditioned chair class with a meal included on most, and on the right routes they beat the road easily. The Gatimaan Express takes one hour forty minutes from Delhi to Agra. Delhi to Amritsar takes about six hours, Chennai to Mysuru around seven and Mumbai to Goa about eight. Executive chair class is worth the small extra cost for the legroom.

We recommend overnight trains selectively. First class AC gives you a lockable two- or four-berth cabin. Second class AC has open berths with curtains and is the best value, while third AC is three-tier and crowded. If a one-hour flight covers the same ground, take the flight. If you want one Indian overnight train for the experience, choose Delhi to Varanasi or Jaipur to Jaisalmer.

Bookings go through IRCTC, the railways' website, which opens reservations 60 days ahead. Foreigners can register, but it's slow with an overseas mobile number and overseas cards often fail at payment. We book for our guests, choose the class and the coach, and have your driver waiting at the station exit at the other end. Porters in red shirts carry bags for a negotiated INR 100 to 200 each.

## The luxury trains

The Maharajas' Express is the flagship: three to six nights through Rajasthan and the Taj corridor, with cabin, meals, excursions and guides included. It's one of the most expensive ways to see India, and fares vary with the cabin and the season, so we quote it on request, with the same route by car alongside for comparison. The Palace on Wheels covers Rajasthan over seven nights, the Deccan Odyssey runs out of Mumbai and the Golden Chariot covers Karnataka and the south. All of them run roughly from October to April.

They suit travellers who want to see a lot in a week without packing and unpacking, people who love trains, and anyone marking a milestone. They're the wrong choice if you like to linger, because the timetable is fixed and the excursions are shared. Three nights on the Maharajas' Express followed by ten days by car gives you some of each.

## Getting around inside cities

Your car and driver are with you in each city too, though some places are better without them. Old Delhi is best by cycle rickshaw and on foot, in comfortable shoes, and the Delhi Metro, with its women-only front coach, is the easiest way to get there. In Varanasi you see the old city and the ghats on foot and by boat. Uber and Ola work in every major city for evenings out, and auto-rickshaws are fine for short hops if you agree the fare first.
