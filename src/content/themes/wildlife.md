India has more wild tigers than any other country, roughly three quarters of all there are, and most of its reserves can be reached in a morning's drive from a city you would visit anyway. That is what makes a wildlife journey here so easy to combine with everything else. Ranthambore is three hours from Jaipur. Kaziranga is a short flight and a drive from Kolkata and Darjeeling. Periyar sits in the hills between Kerala's tea country and its backwaters.

Each park feels different. Ranthambore is dry forest around lakes and the ruins of a thousand-year-old fort, and its tigers are unusually relaxed in the open. Kaziranga is a floodplain of tall grass and shallow lakes on the Brahmaputra, with two thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos, and you'll see a dozen in a morning. Periyar is evergreen forest around a lake, best seen on foot with a tribal tracker or from a boat as elephants come down to drink.

## How we plan a wildlife journey

We put the parks inside a wider route rather than building a whole trip around them, unless wildlife is what you came for. Two or three nights at each park gives you four to six drives, which is enough for a fair chance without the drives blurring into one another. We book the permits early, choose lodges close to the gates, and give you a naturalist who knows the forest.

We're also honest about the odds. A tiger is never guaranteed, and some guests see one on the first drive while others see one on the fifth or not at all. The forest is worth the time either way.

## Who it suits

Families with children who are old enough for early starts, photographers, and anyone who has seen the monuments and wants something wilder. It pairs naturally with Rajasthan's forts, Kerala's hills or the tea gardens of the east.
