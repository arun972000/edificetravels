This route follows India's faiths as much as its foothills. It starts at the Golden Temple in Amritsar, the holiest shrine of the Sikhs, where a kitchen run by volunteers feeds anyone who sits down. It ends at Rishikesh, where the Ganges leaves the mountains and where people have come to study yoga for centuries. In between are the hills: McLeod Ganj, home of the Dalai Lama and the Tibetan government in exile, and Shimla, from which the British ran a fifth of humanity every summer, in a town built to look like Surrey.

The route runs west to east along the foot of the Himalaya and never backtracks: two nights in each of four places, then a last night in Delhi. Two of the drives between the hill towns take seven hours each. They're long days. They are also how you see the foothills properly, and you have the same driver and car throughout.

It's a quieter north than Rajasthan, and cooler. There are no palaces and few forts. Instead you get a gurdwara kitchen, a Tibetan monastery town, a Raj hill station and the Ganges, with time to sit in each.

## Who this journey suits

Travellers interested in religion and ideas, who'll get as much from an hour rolling chapatis in the langar as from any monument. Anyone who wants yoga and the Ganges without signing up for a full retreat. Visitors who have done Rajasthan and want to see the hills. Anyone travelling in April, May or June, when the plains are too hot and the foothills are at their best. It suits solo travellers well, since Amritsar, McLeod Ganj and Rishikesh are among the easiest places in India to be on your own. Not for anyone who needs short drives every day.

## How we adjust it

Add three nights at Ananda in the Himalayas for a proper spa and yoga programme; we'd take a night from Shimla to make room. Add Agra and Jaipur at the end and it becomes a fortnight with the Taj. If you'd rather have mountains than Raj architecture, swap Shimla for the Kullu valley and Manali. Either long drive can be split with an extra night, at Palampur among the tea gardens or at Kasauli, the quietest of the hill stations.
