East of Varanasi, India changes. The food turns to fish and mustard and the language to Bengali, and the pace slows. Beyond Kolkata and the narrow neck of land at Siliguri it changes again, into the seven states of the northeast. Here the Brahmaputra runs several kilometres wide across Assam, the hills are home to some two hundred tribes, and the borders with Bhutan, Tibet, Myanmar and Bangladesh are never far away. Very few foreign visitors to India come this far, and the ones who do have much of it to themselves.

The journey begins in Kolkata, one of the great cities of the world and still, somehow, overlooked. From there you have three choices. A short flight north to Bagdogra takes you to the Darjeeling tea ridge and the monasteries of Sikkim. Fly east to Guwahati instead for Assam, with Kaziranga's rhinos, the monastic island of Majuli and the tea gardens of Upper Assam. Or drive south from Guwahati into Meghalaya's Khasi hills, where villagers have trained bridges out of the roots of living trees and the clouds roll up from Bangladesh every afternoon.

The roads are long and slow, and that's part of the character of the region. Hotels are smaller, you'll stay in more lodges and homestays, and people are more direct with you than they are on the Golden Triangle. We send guests here who have already seen the north, who care about wildlife or tea or textiles, and who want a part of India where they won't meet a coach party.

## How long you need

Seven nights covers Kolkata and Darjeeling, with a stay on a tea estate. Ten adds Kaziranga, with a flight to Guwahati and a night in the tea country. Fourteen gives you Kolkata, Darjeeling and Sikkim's Gangtok and Pelling, then a flight across to Assam for the rhinos and Majuli. Three weeks is the full east, ending with Meghalaya's root bridges. Every one of these journeys begins and ends in Kolkata, and we never give the city less than two nights.
