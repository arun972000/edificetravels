Long-haul visitors to India stay between 20 and 31 days on average, and nearly all of them arrive with a list that's too long. Finding things to include is easy. The hard part of planning a first trip is deciding what to leave out.

## North, south or both?

With 14 days, choose one. With 21, you can do both: two weeks in the north, then a flight south for a slower final week.

The north means Delhi, Agra, Rajasthan and Varanasi: the Taj Mahal, forts and palaces, the desert, the Ganges, and the intensity people picture when they think of India. The south means Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, with backwaters, temple towns, tea hills, coconut-based cooking and a gentler, greener pace.

Fitting both into two weeks means spending a quarter of the trip in airports. If you've been to India before, or you dislike crowds, think about starting with the south on its own.

## The one-direction rule

Never go back to a place just to leave from it. Fly into one city and out of another. Airlines call this an open-jaw ticket, and it rarely costs more. The Rajasthan circuit runs in a natural arc from Delhi through Agra, Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur, and from Udaipur you fly on, either home via Delhi or Mumbai, or south to Kochi. A trip that ends with a six-hour drive back to where it began has wasted a day.

## How many places in how many days?

Divide your nights by three. That's roughly the number of stops to make. Two nights is the minimum anywhere, because a single night is a check-in followed by a check-out. Give three nights to the places you want time in: Jaipur, Udaipur, Kochi, the backwaters and the beach. Agra is the exception. Arrive mid-afternoon, see the Taj Mahal at dawn, and move on.

Count a travel day as half a day lost. Build in one empty afternoon every five or six days for the pool, a massage or a nap. Your arrival day doesn't count as a day.

## A 14-day skeleton for the north

- **Delhi, 2 nights.** Humayun's Tomb, Old Delhi by cycle rickshaw and on foot, and a food walk. Give Delhi more than a stopover.
- **Agra, 1 night.** The Taj Mahal at dawn, Agra Fort, then on.
- **Ranthambore, 2 nights.** Two safaris for tigers, reached via Fatehpur Sikri and the Abhaneri stepwell.
- **Jaipur, 2 nights.** Amber Fort, the old city, and a cooking class or a block-printing workshop.
- **Jodhpur, 2 nights.** Mehrangarh Fort, the blue city and a village drive.
- **Udaipur, 3 nights.** Reached via the Ranakpur temples. Lake Pichola, the City Palace and a day with nothing planned.
- **Delhi or Mumbai, 1 night.** Fly from Udaipur and connect home the next day.

That's 13 nights. If tigers aren't your thing, drop Ranthambore and fly from Udaipur to Varanasi for two nights before flying home from Delhi.

## A 21-day skeleton for north and south

Take the first six stops above (Delhi to Udaipur, 12 nights), then fly from Udaipur to Kochi, connecting through Mumbai or Delhi.

- **Kochi, 2 nights.** Fort Kochi, the spice market, a Kathakali performance and the best seafood on the coast.
- **Munnar, 2 nights.** Tea estates at 1,600 metres, and cool air after the plains.
- **Thekkady, 1 night.** Spice gardens and the Periyar reserve.
- **Backwaters, 1 night.** A private houseboat from Alleppey or a lakeside homestay.
- **Marari beach, 2 nights.** The empty beach you've been promising yourself.

That's twenty nights over 21 days, with two internal flights. With four more nights, add Varanasi before Kerala, or Hampi and Mysuru after it.

## What first-timers regret

We ask every guest what they would change, and the same answers keep coming back.

1. **Too many places.** One-night stops look efficient on paper and blur together in practice. Nobody has ever told us they wished they'd moved more often.
2. **Treating Delhi as transit.** Delhi has the best food in the country, three World Heritage sites and the National Museum, and most people give it an evening.
3. **Missing the Taj Mahal at dawn.** The afternoon crowds and heat spoil it. Stay the night before.
4. **No rest day.** A fortnight of 7 am starts wears everyone out by day nine.
5. **Coming in May.** The plains reach 40°C. Read [our month-by-month guide](/guide/best-time-to-visit-india) before you fix dates.
6. **Choosing hotels by star rating.** A 200-year-old haveli with twenty rooms beats a five-star tower on a ring road. In India, location and character count for more than stars.
7. **Skipping the countryside.** The village drive outside Jodhpur, the stepwell on the way to Jaipur, the tea estates: guests remember these long after the fourth fort.
8. **Overnight trains where a one-hour flight existed.** It sounds romantic, and it costs you a night's sleep.
9. **Underestimating spending money.** Tips, lunches and dinners, and the textile shop you didn't plan to walk into. Our [money and tipping guide](/guide/money-tipping-and-staying-connected) has the figures our guests use.
10. **Not asking.** For a home-cooked meal, a cooking class, a morning in a temple, a tailor. Most of what makes a journey feel like yours is one request away.
