Ask three operators to quote for the same two weeks in India and you'll get three totals that are hard to compare. One includes domestic flights. Another leaves entrance fees to be paid on the day. One names its hotels, and the others say "or similar". This guide covers what moves the cost of a private, tailor-made journey, what a good quote should contain, and how to read several side by side, ours included.

We don't publish prices. Every journey we plan is built around particular dates, hotels and people, and a figure on a web page would be wrong for most of the people reading it. After a short conversation, we send a written quote within 48 hours, itemised line by line, with every inclusion listed and nothing added later.

## What moves the cost of a private India trip?

Mostly the season and the hotels. The number of travellers, the region, how you travel between cities, the guides and experiences you add and the length of the trip all play a part too.

**Season and peak dates.** About 43% of foreign visitors arrive between November and February, and hotels price accordingly. The same palace hotel can cost a good deal more over Christmas and New Year than in late October or March, and some add compulsory gala dinners on 24 and 31 December. Diwali, the Pushkar camel fair and Indian long weekends push rates up locally. If your dates can move a week either side of the peak, that's often the biggest saving available. Our [month-by-month guide](/guide/best-time-to-visit-india) shows where shoulder season pays off.

**Hotel style.** Most of our journeys are planned in three styles: Comfort, Premium and Luxury. The main difference between them is the hotels and room categories, along with the vehicle and how much of each day is private. A heritage haveli with twenty rooms can be better value, and more memorable, than a five-star tower on a ring road. Mixing styles is normal. You might take a modest hotel in Agra, where you sleep one night, and a lake-view room in Udaipur, where you stay three.

**Number of travellers.** A private car, a driver and a city guide cost much the same whether they look after one person or four, so the cost per traveller falls as the group grows. The reverse is the single supplement: a solo traveller pays for the whole room and the whole car. We quote solo journeys individually, because the supplement varies widely from hotel to hotel.

**Region and remoteness.** Rajasthan's palace hotels and Kerala's houseboats are among the larger line items, while Delhi, Agra and the cities of Tamil Nadu offer more for less at an equivalent standard. Ladakh, the Northeast and the far ends of the Himalaya cost more to reach and to supply, the season is short and there are few good hotels to choose from.

**Domestic flights versus driving.** Flying saves days on long legs such as Udaipur to Kochi or Delhi to Varanasi, but fares rise sharply close to departure in peak season and checked baggage allowances are tight. Driving is often the better buy over shorter distances, and it turns a transfer into a day out. Where both make sense, we cost both and tell you which we'd choose.

**Guides and private experiences.** A licensed guide in every city, a historian at Mehrangarh, your own boat on the Ganges, a family dinner in Jaipur, a jeep and naturalist to yourselves in Ranthambore. Each adds to the total, and these are usually what guests remember. A good quote lists them one by one, so you can keep what matters and drop the rest.

**Length of stay.** Longer journeys usually cost less per day, because arrival transfers, first-day guiding and other fixed costs are spread over more nights.

## What should a good quote include?

A quote you can rely on names the hotel and room category for every night, with the meal plan. It states the vehicle, and whether the same driver stays with you throughout. It lists every guide, entrance fee, transfer, flight and train ticket that is included, shows whether GST is inside the total, and sets out the deposit, payment dates and cancellation terms in writing. Exclusions matter as much as inclusions. International flights, visas, insurance, most meals, tips and camera fees should all appear by name.

## How do you compare quotes line by line?

Put every quote into the same currency and the same number of nights, then go through them together, one line at a time.

| What to check in any quote | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Named hotels and room categories | "Four-star or similar" can mean anything, and often means a hotel far from the sights |
| Vehicle, driver and permits | Tourist vehicles carry a yellow plate and an All India Tourist Permit, and one driver should stay with you throughout |
| Guides, and in which cities | A licensed local guide is a different thing from a driver pointing through the windscreen |
| Entrance fees | The Taj Mahal alone costs a foreign visitor INR 1,300 with the mausoleum ticket, and some quotes leave fees to be paid on the day |
| Domestic flights and trains | Check they are included, with baggage, rather than "arranged at extra cost" |
| GST | A quote that adds 5% at payment was 5% higher than it looked |
| Deposit and cancellation terms | A low total with a fully non-refundable deposit carries a risk the figure doesn't show |
| Shopping stops | "Factory visits" and "handicraft centres" are shops that pay the operator a commission |

## What are the red flags in a very cheap quote?

Shopping stops, unnamed hotels, unlicensed drivers, missing guides and pressure to pay quickly. A quote far below the others has to make up the difference somewhere, usually in ways you only find out about on the road.

- **Shopping stops** at carpet, jewellery and textile showrooms that pay the operator a share of whatever you buy.
- **"Similar" hotels**, where the property is confirmed only days before arrival and is rarely the one in the brochure.
- **Unlicensed drivers** in private white-plate cars that aren't insured to carry paying passengers, often changing in every city.
- **No licensed guide**, or entrance fees left for you to pay on the day.
- **Pressure to pay in full**, quickly, or to a personal account.
