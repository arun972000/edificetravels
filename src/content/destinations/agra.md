Everyone has seen the Taj Mahal in photographs, and it makes surprisingly little difference. The pictures get the shape right. They don't prepare you for the size of it once you're through the gateway, or for how quiet the garden is at six in the morning, before the city wakes up. Shah Jahan built it between 1632 and 1653 for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died giving birth to their fourteenth child, and it is the most complete thing the Mughals ever made. We've taken hundreds of guests there and have yet to meet one who came away disappointed.

Agra itself gets a harder press. It's a working city of two million, loud and dusty, and the rickshaw drivers around the monuments are persistent. Our answer is to plan it tightly. Arrive in the early afternoon from Delhi or Jaipur and go straight to Agra Fort, the red sandstone citadel where Shah Jahan spent his last eight years as his son's prisoner, looking at the Taj from a marble balcony. Then cross the river to Mehtab Bagh, the Moonlight Garden, for sunset with the Taj on the other side of the water and no fences in the way. The Taj at dawn the next day, breakfast, and you're on the road.

A second night is worth having. The Itimad-ud-Daulah, built for the father of a Mughal empress a decade before the Taj, is where the inlay craftsmen learned their trade, and it's small enough to take in whole. Akbar's tomb at Sikandra sits in a garden full of blackbuck and langurs. Fatehpur Sikri, the abandoned capital an hour west, shows you an entire Mughal city as it was left in 1585: the emperor's palace, the great mosque and the dry water tanks that explain why everyone walked away. Most tours give Agra a morning. We'd give it a day and a half.
