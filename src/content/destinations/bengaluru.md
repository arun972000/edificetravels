Bengaluru is India's technology capital, a city of thirteen million that doubled in size in a generation and has the traffic to prove it. At 920 metres on the Deccan plateau it's also the mildest big city in the country, with evenings cool enough for a jacket and a habit of gardens, pubs and long breakfasts that goes back to the British cantonment and the Mysore maharajas. Most travellers to Karnataka fly in here, because the airport has the best connections in the south, and the question is how much time to give the city. Our answer is one night, well spent, or none.

The old city is worth a morning. KR Market, under a flyover near the ruins of Tipu Sultan's fort, is a flower market on the scale of a temple festival, and it's at its best between five and seven. Tipu's summer palace, all teak pillars painted red and gold, is round the corner. Lalbagh, the botanical garden Tipu's father laid out in 1760 and the British later enlarged, has a glasshouse modelled on the Crystal Palace and an outcrop of bare rock about three billion years old. Next door, Basavanagudi, the old Brahmin quarter, has the city's bull temple and the eating houses where the benne masala dosa was perfected. We walk all four in a morning.

The afternoon depends on you. The cantonment has Victorian churches and the shaded avenues of Cubbon Park. Indiranagar and Koramangala have the breweries and restaurants of the new city, and Bangalore Palace is a maharaja's version of Windsor Castle. Or you leave after lunch, which is usually what we suggest: Mysore is three hours south-west, Coorg six, Hampi six to the north, and the wildlife lodges on the Kabini river five.
