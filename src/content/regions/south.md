The south surprises people who think they already know India. It's green and wet where Rajasthan is gold and dry. The great temples are places of daily worship, and fifteen thousand people walk through Madurai's on an ordinary Tuesday to pray. The food is rice and coconut, fish and pepper, fermented batters served on banana leaves. Tamil, Malayalam and Kannada belong to a different language family from Hindi altogether. And the pace is slower, which you notice as soon as you step off the plane in Kochi and the air smells of rain and coconut oil in a hot pan.

We think of the south as three journeys that can be joined. Kerala is the hills and the water: tea at Munnar, spice forest at Thekkady, the backwaters at Alleppey, and the sea at the end. Tamil Nadu is temple country, from the rock carvings at Mahabalipuram through French Pondicherry to the Chola heartland at Thanjavur, the mansions of Chettinad and the great goddess temple at Madurai. Karnataka is the plateau, with Mysore's palace, the coffee hills of Coorg and the ruins of Hampi, and Goa waiting on the coast. Any one of them fills a fortnight, and a single mountain road or a short flight takes you from one to the next.

The south is also where we send people who want to get well. Kerala's Ayurveda is a real medical tradition, and the good centres, run by doctors, are the reason to come in the monsoon, when most of the country is too wet to travel.

## How long you need

Twelve days covers Kerala properly: two nights each in Kochi, Munnar and Thekkady, two on the backwaters and three or four by the sea. Fourteen takes you through Tamil Nadu's temple towns from Chennai to Madurai, then over the hills into Kerala to finish on the coast. Ten days in Karnataka links Bengaluru, Mysore, Coorg and Hampi and ends in Goa. With three weeks we join two of these. With a month, you can see the whole of the south without ever feeling rushed.
