Rajasthan is the part of India most people already have a picture of before they arrive, and the picture turns out to be accurate. There are forts on the ridges, and the turbans and saris are every bit as bright as the photographs. What the photographs leave out is everything underneath: the stepwells and painted havelis, the Bishnoi villages where blackbuck graze without fear, and the family kitchens where laal maas has been slow-cooked the same way for ten generations.

We plan Rajasthan in one direction. You usually travel west from Delhi or Jaipur towards the desert and then south to Udaipur, so the landscape changes with every drive and you never cover the same road twice. Some drives are long, five hours across Marwar in places, and we build them into the day with village stops, roadside chai and lunch somewhere you'd never have found on your own.

## How long you need

Ten days covers Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur, with a night in the desert. Twelve lets you begin in Delhi and see the Taj at dawn on the way. Fifteen adds tigers at Ranthambore or the painted towns of Shekhawati. With three weeks, we'd send you on to Kerala afterwards. After the dry gold of the Thar, a few days on the green backwaters is a very good way to finish.
