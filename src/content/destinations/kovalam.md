Kerala's coast runs 580 kilometres from north to south and almost all of it is beach, but most of it isn't for swimming. The sea is rough, the shelves are steep and the fishing villages are working places. Kovalam and Varkala, both within an hour and a half of the state capital, are the two exceptions that became resorts, and they're different enough that the choice matters.

Kovalam is three crescent beaches divided by rocky headlands, the southernmost under a red-and-white lighthouse built in 1972. Lighthouse Beach has lifeguards, a gentle shelf and cafés on the sand, and it's the one place on this coast where we'd happily send a family with small children into the water. It was the first resort in Kerala. Hippies arrived in the 1970s and the charter flights followed, and it shows its age in places. Stay south of the lighthouse, or north at Samudra, where the hotels have gardens and the beach is quieter. Varkala, ninety minutes north, is nothing like it. A red laterite cliff drops straight to the sea, with a path along the top lined with cafés and yoga schools, and below it is the temple beach where Hindu families perform rites for the dead at dawn. The swimming is poor. People come for the sunsets and the walk along the cliff.

Both towns live off Ayurveda, and this is where we urge care. The medical tradition is real and two thousand years old, and a few centres in each place are run by trained doctors who will examine you before anything touches your skin. Many more are massage parlours with a Sanskrit name. For three or four nights at the end of a Kerala route, a daily oil massage and a doctor's consultation are a fine way to finish. For a full course, come for a fortnight in the monsoon. Either way, Thiruvananthapuram airport is twenty minutes from Kovalam when it's time to fly home.
