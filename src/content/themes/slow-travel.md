The most common mistake people make in India is trying to see too much. It's a large country, the distances are real, and a different town every night turns a holiday into a long series of drives and check-ins. Slow travel is the cure: fewer places, more time in each, and a route that never doubles back.

It changes what you remember. On a fast trip you remember the monuments. On a slow one you remember the man who made your chai every morning in Udaipur, the afternoon you spent with a weaver in Chettinad, the evening the houseboat cook showed you how to fry pearl spot in banana leaf. Those things only happen when you stay long enough for them to happen.

Some parts of India are made for it. Kerala's backwaters and tea hills, Tamil Nadu's French town and merchant villages, Coorg's coffee estates and the quiet end of Rajasthan around Jaisalmer and Udaipur all reward a second and third night. In the east, Darjeeling's tea gardens are best seen at walking pace.

## How we plan a slow journey

We draw the route first, as a single line, and then give each stop the nights it deserves. We choose homestays and small hotels run by families where they're the best option, and we leave at least one empty day a week. Drives stay under four or five hours, and where a long leg is unavoidable we fly it or break it with a good lunch.

## Who it suits

Second-time visitors, especially. Couples, friends travelling together and solo travellers who want to settle in rather than tick off. Families with young children, who need a slower pace anyway.
